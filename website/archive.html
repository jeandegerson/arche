Development archive

The early experiments that shaped Archē, from a physics sandbox to a shared world.

01 · v0.0.1–0.1.0

The physical laboratory

The first prototype began with gravity, rigid bodies and touch interaction. Selectable sizes, rods, springs, fixed anchors and torque motors followed. Static platforms and a categorized toolbox made construction possible; local persistence allowed experiments to survive a reload.

02 · v0.1.1–0.2.2

Matter and construction

Geometric cutting conserved mass; attachment points, glue, pegs and curing adhesive introduced ways to join matter. Flexible ropes could be tied, untied and cut. Perforation recovered plugs, while pins relied on physical insertion. Impact fracture, rope overload and elastic or ductile deformation explored material response.

03 · v0.2.3–0.3.3

Natural processes

Thermal exchange, combustion, ash, water, evaporation, freezing and condensation were introduced experimentally. Wind, humidity, solar heating and day-night cycles connected matter to its environment. Geological strata, granular soil, collapse, moisture and clay firing were followed by vegetation growth, seed dispersal and nutrient cycles.

04 · v0.4.0–0.5.3

The human body

The focus shifted to an embodied character with articulated limbs, grasping and muscle controllers. Analog walking, injury and recovery, directional joystick control, climbing, crouching and weight-sensitive pushing and pulling explored the body's relationship to matter.

05 · v0.6.0–0.7.19

An explorable landscape

A following camera, parallax, vegetation and regional habitats transformed the laboratory into a landscape. Sky gradients, clouds, stars, rain, mist and ambient sound established atmosphere. Ponds, birds, butterflies, hares and fireflies populated the environment. Continuous earth and sloped collision replaced visible geological blocks; wading and swimming became possible.

06 · v0.7.20–0.9.50

Movement and geological continuity

Walking, jogging, jumping, crouching and landing were repeatedly refined. Climbing gained alternating handholds, ledge transitions, controlled descent and wall-to-wall jumps. Rectangular cliffs began giving way to mixed-inclination formations. Shared contact geometry, balance, traction and gait controllers aimed to unify movement over soil and rock. Experimental ragdoll changes were partly reverted when ordinary locomotion suffered.

07 · v0.9.51–0.9.62

A broader natural world

Regional meadows, valleys, mountain uplifts and deeper lakes expanded geography. Habitat-based vegetation, seasonal daylight, temperature and evolving weather established natural time. Contextual tree chopping, species and size variation, falling trunks and sound introduced direct interaction with the forest.

08 · v0.9.63–0.9.99

From solitude to shared existence

Realtime character presence began with v0.9.63. Names, remote animations, tree felling and shared-world default access followed. Persistent forest changes, character checkpoints and woodworking snapshots moved the project toward a lasting multiplayer environment. Trunks became logs and planks; carrying, stacking, sawing and persistent fires developed alongside fuel-responsive flames, smoke and sparks.

09 · v0.10–0.11

The official shared landscape

Branches, mixed fire fuel and occupied-hand restrictions extended physical interaction. A new mainland-and-island map replaced the earlier world, with tropical shores, forests, high snowy mountains and lakes. Solo mode was removed from the official entry point. Shared time and weather and multiplayer action synchronization were refined. Cave entrances, chambers and underground traversal became a central development problem.

10 · v0.11.88–0.12.01

A geological field

Material, bedding, fractures, traction and dissolution began to share a geological representation. Overlapping voids and connected underground space were explored. A separate laboratory tested continuous contours, collapse, rock resistance, underground water, rainfall, moisture and root growth; these were experiments, not all confirmed features of the official world. Field-generated cave cross sections entered the shared world in v0.12.01.

11 · v0.12.02–0.12.39

Caves as natural formations

Gradual entrances, buried approaches and lateral erosion replaced artificial ramps. Surface and underground traversal moved toward shared physical boundaries. Cave populations, depth and through-passages expanded; repeated templates were replaced with individually sampled morphology. Exterior relief was decoupled from passage shape. Resistance-guided erosion and branching dissolution explored geological generation rather than decorative cave placement.

12 · v0.12.40–0.12.56

Refining the inhabited world

Cave exits, branch crossings, joystick continuity and floor relief were refined. A conifer chamber prototype informed biome-specific cave chambers. Held-diagonal crouch walking gained planted feet and forward arm balance. Rain was clipped at the surface, and standing trees and felled trunks were grounded against slopes. These are recorded changes, not a guarantee that every issue is resolved.

Latest recorded version: v0.12.56. This journal describes Archē only, not the separate earlier isometric project.
